# InfFyn competitive and commercial assessment

Research accessed September 11, 2026. Primary vendor sources describe advertised capabilities, not an independently tested product comparison. No customer interviews, account trials, outreach, or infrastructure changes were performed. Undated live pages establish what is advertised on the access date; they do not establish historical availability. No future-dated article is used as evidence.

**Follow-up:** [Deeper intake and evidence-workflow research](INTAKE-COMPETITION-2026-09-11.md) adds Revenium's billing imports, strict-ingestion corrections and outcome records, Vantage's selective file converter and **August 20 token-allocation general availability**, and Spendline's detailed close guide. The newer GA evidence supersedes the limited availability assessment in the original Vantage row below.

## Finding

The market need is credible, but the broad positioning is crowded. Cost attribution, customer margin, internal cost-per-outcome, CFO reporting, and even an AI month-close workflow already appear in competitors' official materials. InfFyn should compete on a narrowly useful experience, distribution through fractional CFOs, demonstrable reduction of preparation work, and evidence discipline. It cannot honestly claim that combining AI costs with business value is unique.

The most plausible initial promise is: **A finance-led monthly review of your company's AI economics, using the evidence you already have, with the unresolved amounts and assumptions visible.** This is a positioning hypothesis to validate, not established willingness to pay or a technological moat.

## Demand evidence and its boundary

The FinOps Foundation's 2026 report includes 1,192 respondents representing more than $83 billion of annual cloud spend. It reports 98% managing AI spend; the technology-scope question explicitly includes current and next-12-month management. The sample is 47% large enterprise, 33% enterprise and 20% SMB, with SMB defined as fewer than 1,000 employees. Cost visibility, allocation and value/ROI are cited challenges. [State of FinOps 2026](https://data.finops.org/).

This is directional support for a problem category. It is **not** evidence that 98% of 11–200-person companies buy AI FinOps tools, nor a TAM estimate or InfFyn conversion rate. The report also places 78% of practices under CTO/CIO leadership, which cautions against treating finance as the only buyer or making adoption independent of engineering. Finance sponsorship plus an engineering data owner is the more plausible initial buying group.

## Commercial reasoning for the forecast

These are recommendations and arithmetic, not observed InfFyn customer behavior:

- Treat Stephen's network as a reachable channel with an unknown conversion rate. Do not translate “many contacts” into thousands of qualified prospects.
- Keep one-time onboarding/reconciliation revenue separate from recurring software revenue and from human advisory work. Include the time to clean files, resolve mappings, answer evidence questions and prepare each review.
- A proposed $900 monthly fee is 9% of $10,000 monthly AI spend, 1.8% of $50,000, and 0.9% of $100,000. These ratios do not prove value. At the lower end, a spend dashboard alone faces a difficult comparison with low-cost tools. Higher spend, tangled allocations, repeated board questions or material pricing decisions improve the economic case.
- A three-times-fee value hurdle at $900 would require $2,700/month of defensible customer benefit. That benefit could combine reduced preparation effort and measured economic improvement, but allocated revenue, modeled capacity and lower unit cost must not be counted automatically as realized cash savings.
- Count retention after a second and third monthly review as stronger evidence than initial dashboard enthusiasm. A recurring product fails if users only need a one-off audit.
- The reachable revenue is constrained by activated customers and service capacity: price × retained customers, less acquisition, hosting and delivery costs. More Monte Carlo draws do not improve weak assumptions about those inputs.

## Product evolution with decision gates

1. **Activate and complete first real review.** Reliable sign-in, preservation, company isolation, CSV preparation, reconciliation and report delivery are the immediate requirement. No market research substitutes for hosted acceptance.
2. **Prove repeated monthly usefulness.** Three to five companies complete real reviews. Record preparation time, missing evidence, decisions, and explicit reasons to pay. Seek second/third-cycle continuation. Do not prescribe an arbitrary confidence grade as the primary outcome.
3. **Remove repeated work.** Build/verify the most requested provider and business-outcome imports, reusable allocation mappings, change explanations and follow-up of prior decisions. Trigger this from observed repeated effort rather than connector-count competition.
4. **Add bounded planning.** Scenario comparison for volume, accepted-outcome rate, model mix, price and staffing assumptions once baseline records are dependable. Keep counterfactuals separate from realized benefits. Consider a human-owned decision log with expected impact, owner and later measurement.
5. **Expand advisor distribution; integrate with FynScale in Q2 2027.** Preserve company-owned standalone workspaces, revocable advisor membership, immutable report references and review/release states. Portfolio views are justified only when multiple paying engagements demonstrate a recurring need.

Defer autonomous model routing, request-path enforcement, generalized employee productivity scoring, cross-company benchmark percentiles, and an all-technology finance OS. Each would create a larger competitive and evidence burden than the next useful monthly review.
