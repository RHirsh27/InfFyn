# Northstar Intelligence demonstration

Northstar Intelligence is a fictional 85-person B2B intelligence company. The six completed calendar months, March through August 2026, are a fixed scenario viewed as of September 10, 2026. Every source record, customer, model, and review is synthetic. No provider account is connected.

The demonstration is generated by `engine/app/company_demo.py` through the same monthly calculation, invoice-allocation validation, confidence checks, and comparison engine used by a customer workspace. It does not seed, read, or write a customer database. The public endpoint is `GET /demo/company`; each report also has a read-only evidence export at `GET /demo/company/reports/{report_id}/evidence`.

## The company story

| Month | Included AI and review spend | Product collections associated by management | Unallocated spend |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| March | $22,600 | $48,000 | $900 |
| April | $24,650 | $51,000 | $850 |
| May | $27,100 | $54,000 | $1,000 |
| June | $30,400 | $58,000 | $1,100 |
| July | $35,900 | $60,000 | $1,400 |
| August | $43,400 | $64,000 | $1,900 |

The company has two customer-facing products, Research briefs and Document intelligence, and two internal workloads, Support resolution and Finance document review. Inference and human-review costs are included, including failed and rejected delivery batches. Shared subscriptions remain a separate Unallocated workload. Corporate overhead is outside the defined cost scope.

The August bundled invoice is $64,000, allocated by management as $46,000 to Research briefs and $18,000 to Document intelligence. These are modeled associations of collections. They are not recognized revenue and do not establish that AI caused revenue.

## What the reviewer should discover

- Research brief accepted volume grows from 960 to 3,080 while cost per accepted brief falls from about $8.33 to $3.96.
- Document intelligence accepted volume rises from 4,000 to 9,000 while cost rises from $6,800 to $19,200. Its August associated collections remain $18,000, producing a $1,200 contribution loss within the included cost scope. More rejected batches and a rising review-cost share suggest the next investigation, rather than proving a technical cause.
- Support resolves 7,200 accepted cases in August, compared with 3,000 in March, while cost per accepted resolution falls from $1.60 to about $0.93. This is an efficiency change, not verified cash savings.
- August Finance document review includes $3,400 of cost against a $3,620 control. Its $220 discrepancy remains visible and reduces the cost-evidence checklist score. It should prompt reconciliation before financial decisions.
- August Unallocated spend is $1,900. Its assignment check remains incomplete even though the included amount reconciles to its supplied control.

Accepted quantities count business units; acceptance rates count delivery batches. The workload acceptance definitions explain that distinction. Internal capacity estimates use synthetic end-to-end before/after labor minutes and loaded rates. Those estimates cover more employee work than the incremental review expense included in the cost ledger; they are modeled capacity, never realized cash savings.

## Engineering checks

`engine/.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/test-engine-safe.py engine/tests/test_company_demo.py -q`

The tests verify six-month totals and dimensional reconciliation, growth and unit efficiency, the negative product contribution, visible confidence gaps, exact invoice allocation, complete failed-batch denominators, deterministic bounded output, read-only HTTP behavior, and identical recalculation from exported evidence.

The demo is useful for evaluating the product flow and explaining the intended financial decisions. It does not prove hosted authentication, database isolation, provider access, live imports, customer demand, or willingness to pay.
